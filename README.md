<h1><strong>What Are the Best Natural Ways to Care for Your Nails?&nbsp;</strong></h1>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">To take care of your nails naturally, keep them clean and dry, trim them straight across, file out snags, moisturize your hands and cuticles, limit water and chemical exposure, and eat a balanced diet.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">These simple habits matter more than any supplement. Nails grow slowly, so healthy nails come from steady care over weeks and months, not a one-day fix.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This guide covers a natural nail care routine, how to clean fingernails and the area under them, what causes brittle nails, whether vitamins such as biotin help, how fast nails grow and when a nail problem needs a doctor.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It is general information, not medical advice.</span></p>

<h2><strong>Healthy Nails vs Unhealthy Nails</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Normal nails are smooth, slightly curved and evenly colored, usually pink under the nail plate with a pale half-moon near the base in some people.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Fingernails and toenails differ a little, but both should be firm, not painful.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Signs of unhealthy nails include:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Yellow, brown or green discoloration</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Thick, crumbly or lifting nails</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Deep ridges, pits or splits</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Swelling, redness or pain around the nail</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dark streaks that appear without injury</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I did not verify each of these from a single source, so treat the list as general guidance.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The American Academy of Dermatology says nails that change, swell or cause pain can signal serious nail problems and are worth a dermatologist's visit.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Healthy nails vs unhealthy is mostly about change: a new look, shape or feel.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Nail Care Routine and Nail Maintenance</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The American Academy of Dermatology's</span><a href="https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/nail-care-secrets/basics/healthy-nail-tips"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">11 tips for healthy nails</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, last updated February 10, 2025, make a solid nail care routine. Highlights:</span></p>
<ol>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Keep nails clean and dry, since moisture lets bacteria and fungi grow under the nail.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Trim straight across with sharp clippers and slightly round the corners.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">File with an emery board to remove snags.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Don't bite your nails or remove your cuticles.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Don't use your nails as tools.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Limit water exposure, wear gloves for dishes and cleaning, and moisturize after washing hands.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Trim toenails regularly and wear properly fitting shoes.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Wear flip-flops in public showers and at pools to lower fungal risk.</span></li>
</ol>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For thick toenails, the page suggests soaking them in warm salt water (1 teaspoon of salt per pint) for 5 to 10 minutes before cutting, and says not to dig out ingrown toenails, especially infected or sore ones.&nbsp;</span></p>

<h2><strong>How to Clean Nails and Under Your Nails</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">How to clean your nails starts with soap, warm water and a soft nail brush, and good fingernail health starts with the same habit.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For how to clean under nails and how to clean fingernails, gently scrub under the free edge, rinse and dry well, then moisturize.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Avoid sharp tools that can scratch the skin under the nail or push dirt deeper.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dirty fingernails can trap germs, so wash your hands and nails after gardening, cooking and using the bathroom.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dry nails thoroughly, since the AAD notes damp conditions help germs grow. Cleaning your nails and keeping healthy fingernails go hand in hand.</span></p>
<h2><strong>What Causes Brittle Nails?</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Many readers ask why my nails are so brittle and what causes brittle nails.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Common causes include frequent hand washing, harsh cleaning products, nail polish remover, constant wet-dry cycles, picking, injury and age.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">These points come from general knowledge, not from a source I checked in this session. Brittle nails can sometimes signal a health issue such as thyroid problems or iron deficiency, so persistent changes deserve a doctor's check.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">On brittle nails vitamin deficiency, the AAD page I read does not discuss it, and I did not find a source in this session linking specific vitamin deficiencies to brittle nails.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A blood test can tell you if you are low in anything.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Best Vitamins for Nails: What About Biotin?</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Biotin is the vitamin most often named when people search for the best vitamins for nails or how to strengthen fingernails.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">GoodRx's</span><a href="https://www.goodrx.com/conditions/hair-loss/biotin-for-hair-and-nails-a-waste-of-your-time"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">biotin article</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, updated April 1, 2025, says nail studies are limited.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Trials found that 2.5 mg of biotin daily may improve nail firmness, hardness and thickness, but the studies are "small and not of the highest quality."&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It adds that most people on a Western diet get enough biotin from food, and the adequate intake for nonpregnant adults is 30 mcg a day.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">There is also a safety warning.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The article cites a 2019 FDA update saying biotin can interfere with certain lab tests, including some thyroid hormone and vitamin D tests and troponin, a heart attack marker.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Tell your doctor if you take biotin before bloodwork.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">So the best vitamin for nails may be none: fix any real deficiency, and ask your doctor before taking high doses.</span></p>

<h2><strong>When to See a Doctor</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">See a doctor or dermatologist if nails change shape or color, swell, hurt, separate from the nail bed or show dark streaks.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The AAD also says people with diabetes or poor circulation should see a doctor for any nail problem, since treatment matters more for them.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Seek prompt care for redness, warmth, pus or fever around a nail.</span></p>
